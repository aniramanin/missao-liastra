Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer

Teacher guide

How to use the Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer in a 45 to 50 minute lesson. It runs in the browser, with no sign-up, on computers, tablets and phones.

Open the game

Learning objectives

  1. Explain why, at a base beyond Earth, every choice has a cost: power used by one system is missing from another.
  2. Describe how solar radiation threatens the crew and how water and regolith work as shields.
  3. Understand that the communication delay with Mars forces the crew to decide on its own.
  4. Connect plants, light and oxygen: the greenhouse is food and also life support.
  5. Read and trust real data: the game’s space weather comes from the real Sun (NASA DONKI).

Lesson plan (45 to 50 min)

5 minOpening question“If you lived on Mars, what would run out first: air, water, food or power?” Write the answers on the board.
25 minMission in pairsEach pair picks a mission from the table below. One student plays, the other reads the checklist and the energy panel; they swap halfway.
10 minQuiz and reportAt the end of the mission, each pair answers the 3-question challenge and uses the “Copy a summary for class” button.
5 to 10 minWrap-up talkCompare the pairs’ choices using the discussion questions.

Discussion questions

  • Which choice looked safe but cost a lot later?
  • Why is the radiation dose on the Moon higher than on Mars?
  • If a message to Earth takes 11 minutes, which decisions must the crew be able to make alone?
  • Besides food, what does the greenhouse give back to the base?
  • What would you do differently on the next mission?

How to assess

In-game quiz
At the end of each mission there are 3 questions (2 about the mission’s world and 1 general). The score goes into the copied summary.
Pair report
The summary includes the result, stars, science, radiation, harvests, tasks completed and the mission’s real basis.
Simple rubric
Beginning: describes what happened. Developing: explains a trade-off between two resources. Mastering: justifies a decision with a real number from the game.

Unplugged activity: the 6 cells

Print the table and give each group 6 tokens. A solar storm is coming: the group splits the tokens among the systems and explains its choice. Then reveal what each system does (like the game’s energy panel).

SystemWhat a token doesGroup tokens
Life supporteach token keeps air and water going; below 3, air starts to run out 
Greenhouse (LED light)each token grows the plants and makes food and oxygen 
Water shieldup to 2 tokens cut radiation inside the base; more does not help 
Scienceeach token makes discoveries but keeps no one alive 

Where the data comes from

Space weather: NASA DONKI (live, with a real backup archive). Radiation: Curiosity/RAD on Mars (0.67 mSv per day) and Chang’e 4 on the Moon (1.37 mSv per day). Earth–Mars delay: JPL orbital elements. Image of the Sun: NASA SDO. Life support: NASA, Baseline Values and Assumptions Document. What is simplified (sped-up time, the 50 mSv training limit) is explained on the website.
